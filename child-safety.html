Child safety standards

Aura365Life — standards against child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE)

Last updated: 16 August 2026

Aura365Life is a location-sharing app for families and friends, with a minimum age of 13. Because

it is used by teenagers, child safety is not a compliance exercise here — it is the constraint the

product is designed around. This page sets out our standards, how to report a concern, and what we

do about it.

1. What is prohibited

The following are banned outright, and an account found responsible is removed permanently and

without warning:

  • Child sexual abuse material (CSAM) of any kind.
  • Sexualising a child in any way, including in a display name, group name, group topic, or a status

message.

  • Grooming, or any attempt to establish a relationship with a child for sexual purposes.
  • Soliciting, arranging or attempting to arrange a meeting with a child for sexual purposes.
  • Sextortion — threatening to share sexual imagery to coerce a child.
  • Trafficking of a child, or advertising a child for exploitation.
  • Any attempt to use a child's location, shared through this app, to locate, follow, approach or

contact them without their consent.

The last one is specific to what this app does, and we treat it as seriously as the rest.

2. How the product is built to reduce risk

These are design decisions, not settings, and they cannot be turned off by a user or by us:

  • A stranger cannot see any individual's location. Precise location is visible only inside a

private circle a person has joined by one-time invite code. Public groups never show an

individual — presence there is aggregated on our servers, snapped to a coarse grid roughly

1.2km × 0.6km, and suppressed entirely unless at least five people are in that cell. There is

no view anywhere in the product in which a stranger can resolve a specific child's position.

  • There is no public directory of people. You cannot search for a user, browse nearby users, or

see who is close to you. There is no mechanism to discover a child by proximity.

  • There is no messaging. The app has no free-text chat. Contact between users is limited to a

fixed vocabulary of preset one-tap signals. This removes the primary channel through which

grooming occurs.

  • No photo uploads. Avatars and clan crests are assembled from a fixed set of preset parts.

Users cannot upload images, so the product cannot be used to distribute imagery.

  • Location history is deleted after 7 days, automatically, and no history of coarse group

presence is retained at all.

  • Anyone can leave any circle at any time, in one tap, and no one can prevent them.
  • Age gate at sign-up. A neutral date-of-birth entry is required; under-13s are refused.

3. How to report

In the app. Every user profile and every group has a report option. The reporting flow offers,

among other categories, "Adult or sexual content" and "I'm worried about someone's safety".

Reports can be made by anyone who can see the content, including a child reporting about

themselves, and no free-text is required.

Users can also block another user from inside the app, which takes effect immediately and does

not require a report to be filed first.

4. What we do about a report

1. Reports are reviewed. Every report raised in the app enters a moderation queue reviewed by

the operator of the service. We aim to review reports within 72 hours, and we prioritise

anything categorised as a safety concern or sexual content ahead of everything else.

2. Action is taken on the account. Where a report is upheld, the account is removed. For

anything involving CSAE the removal is permanent and immediate, and we do not require the

reporter to justify it further.

3. CSAM is reported to the authorities. Where we identify, or are made aware of, apparent child

sexual abuse material or conduct constituting an offence, we report it to the appropriate

authority — in the United Kingdom that is CEOP/the National Crime Agency, and the IWF for

imagery — and we preserve the associated account records for them rather than deleting them.

4. We do not tip off the account. A person reported for CSAE is not told who reported them, or

what was said.

5. Where we are, and what we are not

Aura365Life is operated by a sole developer in the United Kingdom, and is available in the United

Kingdom only. We state this plainly rather than implying a moderation capability we do not have:

there is no 24-hour moderation team, and review is performed by one person.

The product is designed on the assumption that this is true. The features most often used to harm

children on a social product — open discovery, free-text messaging, image upload, and visible

individual location to strangers — are absent by design rather than moderated after the fact,

because removing the mechanism is the only control that works reliably at this scale.

Aura365Life is not a child safety service, a monitoring service, or a substitute for one. It

does not promise that a parent will always be able to see where their child is, and it is not

designed for covert monitoring — sharing is visible to the person sharing, and they can always

stop.
