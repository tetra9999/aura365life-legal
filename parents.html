For parents

Your child has installed Aura365Life, or is asking to. This page explains what it does, what it

deliberately does not do, and where it will disappoint you — because the last one is the part you

actually need.

Last updated: 12 August 2026

The one thing to take away

This app shows a phone, not a person.

Phones get switched off. They run out of battery, lose signal, sit in a bag at the bottom of a

locker, and get left at a friend's house. Someone can be completely fine and not appear on the map.

Please do not use this app to keep someone safe. If you are worried about your child, contact

them, or contact the police. Nothing here is a substitute for either, and treating it as one is the

most dangerous thing you could do with it.

What it does

Your child appears on a map to the people in their circles — small, private groups joined only

by a single-use invite code. Nobody can add themselves. Nobody can add your child. You cannot be

put into a circle without someone sharing a code with you and you entering it.

There is also a public side — groups, which have a game layer with clans and territory. Public

groups never show individual people, only crowd counts across areas about a kilometre wide, and

only where at least five people are present. Your child's position is never visible to a stranger.

That distinction is the whole product: circles put you on the map, groups don't.

Where this app will disappoint you

Stated plainly, because you will find these out eventually and it is better you find them here.

Your child can leave, at any time, and you cannot stop them

Leaving any circle is one tap. It is never blocked, and there is no parental override — not by

design oversight, by decision.

For family circles, the departure is recorded and shown to the circle, so you will see it the

next time you open the app. You will not be able to prevent it.

You will not be notified of anything

There are no push notifications in this app at all, and there never will be. Not when your child

leaves a circle, not when they arrive somewhere, not when they stop sharing.

This is the decision most likely to frustrate you, so here is the reasoning rather than just the

rule. An app that pings a parent the moment a teenager does something becomes an alarm system, and

teenagers evade alarm systems — a second phone, the phone "forgotten" at a friend's house, a

spoofing app. The documented response to being monitored is not compliance; it is evasion. **An app

your child actually keeps on their phone is worth more than one that alerts you until they delete

it.**

There are no place alerts, and no history for you to browse

No geofencing. No "arrived at school". No "left home at 4pm". None of it exists, and none of it is

planned.

Location history is kept for 7 days and then deleted automatically by the database. It is

visible to circle members as a recent route, not as a searchable log of your child's life.

In friends circles, your child can hide from their friends

Friend circles have a Ghost Mode. When it is on, your child simply stops appearing — and to

everyone else that looks exactly like a flat battery or no signal, which is deliberate. A teenager

who has to explain why they hid has not really been given privacy.

Ghost Mode does not work in family circles. In a family circle your child is visible whenever

their phone is sharing. They can leave, or turn location off entirely at the phone level, but they

cannot stay in the circle and be invisible.

We think you should know both halves of that, rather than only the reassuring one.

Things worth knowing before you agree

Three honest limitations, none of which are secret but all of which are easy to miss:

1. Age is self-declared. We ask for a date of birth and enforce 13+, but we do not verify it.

Someone could enter a false date, in either direction.

2. Clan rosters are public. If your child joins a public clan, their display name is visible to

any signed-in user browsing that clan, and the clan's territory indicates roughly the area it

operates in. There is no way for anyone to message or contact your child from there — the app has

no messaging at all — but the name is visible. If that concerns you, your child can stay out of

public clans entirely and use circles only.

3. We cannot guarantee always-on sharing to you. Family circles are designed so that sharing

stays on, but that is a product guarantee, not a technical one. A determined teenager can turn

off the phone's location permission, or leave the phone behind. We would rather tell you that

than sell you a certainty the app cannot deliver.

Talking to your child about it

Not our business, and one observation anyway, because the product is built on it: **the same dot on

the same map means opposite things depending on who chose to put it there.** Sharing your child

agreed to is a sign of trust between you. Sharing imposed on them is monitoring, and it tends to

produce exactly the secrecy it was meant to prevent.

The most useful thing this app can give you is not a location. It is the ordinary, low-stakes sense

of your family being around — which is also, incidentally, what teenagers say they want from it.

Deleting the account

Your child can delete their account from inside the app, at any time, and everything goes: profile,

location history, circle and group memberships, and their live position. The full description, and

what to do if the app has already been uninstalled, is on the

account deletion page.
